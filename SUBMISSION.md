# Devpost submission draft: Antidote

Track: Best Apps and Agents. Bonus: Best Use of Tavily. Team: Ujwal Vanjare (code), teammates on testing and video.

## Tagline
Reads a pesticide label, names the chemical and its WHO hazard class, retrieves the cited antidote guidance or stops when there is none, and fills the medico-legal register. Decision support for trained clinicians on open NVIDIA Nemotron models.

## The result, in time
On 16 July 2013, 23 children died in Gandaman, Bihar, after a school lunch cooked in oil kept in a pesticide container; police named the chemical, monocrotophos, from the forensic report four days later (Al Jazeera, 21 July 2013). In 2017 in Yavatmal, about 800 cotton farm workers were admitted to hospital after spraying pesticides and more than 20 died between July and October, before the state ordered an investigation at the beginning of October (Public Eye, https://toxicexports.publiceye.ch/). Antidote names the chemical from the label in under 30 seconds, with its WHO hazard class and quoted national guidance, for about 4 US cents, and its register flags a spraying cluster at the third case in a week. Where poison-centre guidance reaches the bedside, patients leave hospital 3.43 days sooner on average (Galvão et al., Sao Paulo Medical Journal 2011); Antidote puts that guidance on every card.

## First sentence (two numbers)
On 55 published poisoning cases and 68 photos of real Indian pesticide labels, Antidote produced no card that would point a doctor the wrong way, and set right 10 of the 12 cases where the treating team first mistook the poison, usually for an organophosphate.

## Inspiration
In 94 percent of recorded acute pesticide poisonings in India the chemical went unidentified (PAN India analysis of government data, 2026). The answer was printed on the bottle in the family's hand: every Indian label legally carries the active ingredient, a colour-coded toxicity triangle and an antidote statement. Paraquat kills 50 to 90 percent of patients in Indian hospitals, and the first error is treating it like an organophosphate.

## What it does
The family brings the bottle. A doctor photographs the label. Antidote reads the fields, matches the government registry, looks up the WHO hazard class and whether an antidote exists, and assembles a bedside card made only of sentences quoted word for word from the national poison-management module and the ISCCM 2024 statement, each with its page. Where no antidote exists a stop card appears first. Lethal errors such as atropine for paraquat are blocked by rules. Nemotron 3 Ultra audits the card against both guideline documents in full. The medico-legal register row is pre-filled from the label. The register then becomes an early-warning signal: rules flag a spraying cluster, a banned chemical still in circulation and restricted-use chemicals, and Nemotron 3.5 Lightning reads the whole register plus the full text of every published case report on those chemicals (about 194,000 tokens, about one cent) to write the district officer's brief. Hermes Agent runs the watch daily and a Tavily ban watch weekly.

## How we built it
Nebius Token Factory: Nemotron 3 Nano normalises label text, Nemotron 3 Super composes under a quote-only schema, Nemotron 3 Ultra audits with both documents in a one-million-token context; NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Super VL reads the label and a second, different model reads it again; the chemical is used only when both reads agree and it appears in the text read, so no single model's misreading of small print can reach the doctor. Offline, NVIDIA Nemotron Nano 12B v2 VL reads labels on a laptop GPU. Tavily resolves unknown brands and fetched the WHO classification and the CIB&RC tables that block automated downloads. The WHO 2019 table was parsed into 649 chemicals with source lines. A deterministic rules engine and an exact-span verifier decide what reaches the clinician. Antidote is also an MCP server registered with Hermes Agent. A Hermes agent also runs in an NVIDIA NemoClaw sandbox on Nemotron 3 Super through Token Factory, with only a placeholder key and no open web. On Nebius AI Cloud, a Serverless Job verified both entries from a clean clone (PASS), and a Serverless Endpoint served NVIDIA Nemotron Nano 12B v2 VL for a single-reader run of the label benchmark, which showed why Antidote pairs two independent reads with a brand check: together they give 0 unsafe cards in 68. Same platform as Sieve, our other entry, with a different domain, practitioner, corpus and record.

## How Antidote uses Tavily (Best Use of Tavily)
- **A safety check, not a search box.** When the two vision reads of a label disagree or a brand is unknown, Tavily searches registry and retailer pages for that brand and counts which registered chemicals they name. Antidote acts only on a clear winner. This check is part of why the label benchmark has 0 unsafe cards in 68 real photos.
- **Got the data that blocks robots.** WHO's 2019 hazard classification and the Indian CIB&RC tables refuse automated downloads; Tavily extract fetched them, and they became the 649-chemical table behind every hazard class.
- **Keeps the register legally current.** A weekly Hermes job uses Tavily to search government notices for newly banned or restricted pesticides, because a ban missed would put the wrong regulatory line on a medico-legal record.
- **Built the evaluation.** Tavily search and extract collected the 55 open-access case reports and the 68 real label photos used in the benchmarks.

## Validated against real-world evidence
Antidote is checked against what treating doctors have already published: 55 open-access poisoning case reports, each run through Antidote and compared with the authors' own management by two independent judges, and 68 photos of real Indian pesticide labels. Its clinical text comes only from the NHSRC poison-management module and the ISCCM 2024 position statement, quoted word for word with the page, and its lethal-error rules are readable by anyone.

### What the published evidence says

- **Doctors treat the wrong poison when nobody can name it, and the bottle settles it.** In an Indian paraquat series, many patients could not name the agent, so initial treatment was directed at organophosphorus poisoning: before referral they received atropine, pralidoxime and oxygen, and "oxygen therapy is known to cause free radical injury and increased mortality in these patients". The diagnosis was made from the container, or from pictures of it shared on WhatsApp by relatives (Dambal et al., National Medical Journal of India 2021, https://nmji.in/reasons-for-under-reporting-of-paraquat-poisoning-in-india/). That photo is exactly what Antidote reads.
- **The toxidrome alone misleads.** In a Bangladesh hospital series, 63 of 198 patients (31.8 percent) were misdiagnosed from the clinical toxidrome; "identification of the specific compound is essential for anticipating and reducing complications, administering appropriate and timely management" (Verma, Lamb, Sattar, Ghose, Eddleston, Trans R Soc Trop Med Hyg 2024, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149373/).
- **The product decides the outcome.** Case fatality ranges from around 70 percent for paraquat and aluminium phosphide to close to 0 percent for many newer pesticides; WHO tells families to "take along the bottle" (WHO, Clinical management of acute pesticide intoxication, 2008, https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/44020).
- **The specific chemical is rarely recorded.** A systematic review of lethal poisoning in India found pesticides behind 94.5 percent of the poisoning deaths studied, yet "few papers identified the specific organophosphorus insecticide responsible" (Karunarathne et al., BMC Public Health 2021, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-021-11156-2).
- **Highly hazardous products are still on the shelf.** Of 318 pesticides registered in India in 2019, 18 were WHO class Ia or Ib (Bonvoisin et al., BMC Public Health 2020, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12889-020-8339-z). Antidote shows the WHO class and the Indian regulatory status on every card.
- **Expert guidance is scarce, and it works.** Most emergency physicians surveyed in south and east India reported having no poison centre, and most there were unaware of the national hotline (Blanchard et al., Toxicology Communications 2019, https://doi.org/10.1080/24734306.2019.1635725). Where poison-centre guidance is available, patients stay in hospital 3.43 days less on average (Galvão et al., Sao Paulo Medical Journal 2011, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10865900/). Antidote puts the national guideline's own sentences, and the poison-centre number, on every card.

## Where Token Factory accelerated our workflow

- Five NVIDIA models behind one key and one API shape. Nemotron 3 Nano normalises labels, 3 Super assembles cards, 3 Ultra audits them, 3.5 Super VL reads photos and 3.5 Lightning writes the district brief, all through the same OpenAI-compatible call. Moving a stage between tiers is a one-line change.
- An auditor that reads both national guidelines at once. Nemotron 3 Ultra checks every card against the complete NHSRC module and ISCCM 2024 statement in one long-context call. Hosting a 550B model ourselves was out of reach.
- Evaluation we could repeat until it was right. The case-report benchmark, the label-photo benchmark and the negative controls each cost cents to a few dollars per run, so we reran them after every safety fix and every run is in the repository.
- A vision model appeared mid-hackathon and we switched in a day. Nemotron 3.5 Super VL arrived in the catalog; adopting it took a model id and one request flag, and it fixed label misreads the previous reader made.
- A whole district in one call. The surveillance brief sends the register, the alerts and 32 full case reports, about 194,000 tokens, to Nemotron 3.5 Lightning for about one cent, so the district officer gets an explanation, not just a count.
- One ledger. Every call records tier, model, tokens, dollars and latency, shown to the doctor in the Trace panel. That transparency came almost free because every model sits behind one endpoint.

## Evaluation (reproducible with `make eval`)
Lethal-error sentences blocked by rules alone 10 of 10. No-antidote labels: stop card 20 of 20, atropine-free cards 20 of 20. Non-pesticides routed away from antidotes 10 of 10. Organophosphates never falsely stopped 10 of 10. WHO class agreement 96.9 percent. Citation integrity 100 percent: any quote not found word for word on its page is replaced by the page itself. Published case reports: 55 open-access poisoning case reports in two pre-registered sets, verified quote by quote, ground truth from the paper rather than from Antidote's registry. On the live code: 0 unsafe cards, 44 specific and correct, 10 of 12 documented misidentifications set right, every other case held safely for confirmation; two independent judges found 83.6 and 98.1 percent of cards consistent with the treating doctors' management. When only the class is legible and the clinician records cholinergic signs, a class-level rule gives the right guidance (2 of 2 held-out cases). Real label photos: 68 photos of Indian pesticide bottles from product pages, annotated by eye before any run: 0 unsafe cards, 49 read correctly (83.3 percent when the chemical is legible), the rest held for the doctor to confirm from the bottle. Four deterministic safeguards make this hold: two independent vision reads that must agree, a grounding gate so no chemical reaches the registry unless it was actually read, a Tavily check of the brand's registered composition, and a registry that decides whether the antidote appears on the card. Against a general model (pre-registered): Nemotron 3 Super asked directly on the same 55 cases, with the chemical name typed in for it, wrote 107 drug doses with no source in 47 of 55 answers and never held back when the product was unknown; Antidote's cards contain only guideline sentences with their pages. A general model knows the textbook. In a real casualty ward the missing piece is the chemical, and that is what Antidote reads from the bottle.

## Prior art and what is new
webPOISONCONTROL (US consumer barcode triage) and SprayWise (student label reader for farmers). Nothing reads the bottle for the doctor, matches the Indian registry and WHO class, composes from quotes only, stops on lethal errors, and fills the register.

## Challenges
Government and WHO sites block automated downloads; Tavily extract solved it. Parsing a 90-page classification into a table with source lines. Keeping a language model from ever writing a dose: solved with a schema that separates quotes from explanations and a gate that removes drug names and numbers from explanations.

## What is next
Regional-language brand aliases, more state protocols, and deployment with a poison-information centre. Hospital stock lists and local protocols already work per workspace.
